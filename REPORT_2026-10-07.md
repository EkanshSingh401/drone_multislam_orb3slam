## Step 4 — optimizer's curse: chosen goals are over-predicted; the score does not predict realized gain
λ = 0.01, new set, 3 flights, 30% of goals picked uniformly at random (`random_pick`). Realized =
change of log det of OpenVINS's IMU pose covariance from the decision to arrival (65 goals reached,
10 not reached and skipped).

| pick | n | predicted dI_pose (median) | realized (median) | predicted − realized, median [95% CI] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| argmax | 43 | 3.20 | 0.076 | **+3.05 [2.77, 3.27]** |
| random | 22 | −1.27 | 0.026 | −1.69 [−2.63, 0.77] |

One-sided Mann-Whitney (argmax over-predicted more than random): p = 7e-9. **Yes — chosen viewpoints
are systematically over-predicted.** But the stronger, simpler result: at goal scale (~5 s of travel)
the prediction does not track the realized change at all (Spearman pred vs realized 0.04, p 0.73,
n = 65; measurement part 0.08). The predicted measurement gain is ~5 nats, while OpenVINS's pose
covariance barely changes (0.08 nats): the predictor keeps the in-state landmarks as persistent
anchors along the path, whereas the sliding-window filter marginalizes them. The 1 s-segment
agreement of §61–62 (1.09x) does not extend to the planning horizon. The over-prediction of chosen
goals is then largely selection on a high-variance, non-predictive score (the curse in its plainest
form). Paper-relevant, with that caveat: one scene, one λ, 3 flights; the random arm is small (22).
