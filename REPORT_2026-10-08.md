One step is right (meas. gain ratio 1.06, propagation 0.049 vs 0.052 nats; the §61–62 agreement),
and the model's propagation loss is close at every horizon (0.65 nats/s on the 1506 goals vs 0.67–0.70
realized). The model's *measurement* information from one snapshot grows ~2x faster than the filter's
from 0.5 s on. Realized, the pose covariance is at an equilibrium: per 5 s propagation removes
3.6–4.0 nats and updates put back the same amount (net +0.02–0.07).
Where the excess sits (2 s horizon): ~70% outside the local roll/pitch block, i.e. in yaw/position,
the directions a VIO filter cannot observe; roll/pitch itself is over-predicted 1.6x (1.65 vs 1.02
nats). The excess is largest when the vehicle **rotates with little translation** (1 s: model/realized
3.3 in the top rotation tertile vs 1.3 with no rotation; 3.5 in the lowest translation tertile vs 1.2
in the highest) — not a parallax effect. **FEJ is not the explanation**: replaying the same flight with
`use_fej: false` (diagnostic) leaves the filter's realized flows unchanged (net 0.044 vs 0.041 nats/s,
same split), so linearization-point differences do not create the extra information in the filter.
Remaining candidate (untested): the predictor propagates with a static, non-rotating IMU model
(synthetic level, unaccelerated) and maps clone errors to the waypoint attitude only inside the
measurement Jacobians; under rotation the real error dynamics (gyro-bias and gravity coupling in a
rotating frame) differ. Next test: predictor with the flown IMU propagation (sensor flights) instead of
the static model.

**b) Realized information by source** (stage log, airborne, two flights): SLAM-landmark updates

+0.2 to +0.3, init ~0. Landmarks leaving the window cost the model little: persistent vs windowed
landmarks on the same path differ by 0.05 nats median (IQR 0.01–0.14). Marginalization changes the
pose marginal by exactly 0 (as it must).

**c) What carries signal for realized Δlogdet** (1506 goals; Spearman [95% CI over flights];

visible GT surface in the FOV at arrival (emulated depth rays ≤ 8 m) ρ = 0.25 [0.21, 0.30], R² 0.01;

mostly equilibrium noise; nothing simple predicts them well, and the predicted gain least of all.

**Safety finding (3 of 3 flights): the 0.05 rad/s in-place rotation failed in flight.** ~20 s into it
(t ≈ 208–212 s), live OpenVINS's position drifted from GT (0.7 m in flight 1 while it reported
standing still), then jumped 0.68 / 0.15 / 0.39 m in a single 4–8 ms step; the VIO health gate opened
(working as designed: jump → dropout); in flights 1 and 3 the sim-only watchdog also cut the motors at
1.6 m, in flight 2 PX4's failsafe descent landed it. The 0.15 / 0.4 rad/s segments were therefore
flown only in flight 2's script after the failure (no estimate) — not usable. Streams were regular
throughout (images 30 Hz, IMU and odometry 200 Hz) and OpenVINS was 3 ms behind: not a stall. The
offline replay reproduces the drift on flights 1 and 3 (error 0.1 → 1.1 m between t = 200 and 210 s),
not on flight 2.

**c) Why, with matched noise.** Two isolating experiments:
1. *OpenVINS's own simulator* (`run_simulation`) driven by the flights' GT trajectories, with our
   estimator config, rig calibration and IMU noise, ideal 1 px features: roll/pitch NEES **1.1**
   (d4_sens_lam0_1 low-rotation trajectory), **1.4** (a frontier trajectory), **0.16** (d5_lowrot_1,
   including the failing rotation; position error ≤ 1.7 cm). No rise at low rotation (0.8 below
   0.02 rad/s). **The filter itself — FEJ, accel-bias/gravity coupling, initialization — is consistent
   on these motions.** (FEJ off also leaves the realized information flows unchanged, step 5.)
2. *Same recorded data, fewer frames* (diagnostic only, not adopted): replaying at 5 Hz tracking
   instead of the system's every-other-frame rate drops the slow-rotation roll/pitch NEES from
   6.7 / 15.1 / 37.5 to **0.7 / 3.2 / 4.8**, the later legs and hover to 1.0–3.8, and **removes the drift
   and jump** (flight 3: error stays 0.05–0.08 m through t = 200–210 s, vs 0.1 → 1.1 m).

With the IMU near-matched (a) and the filter consistent on ideal data (c1), the remaining input is
vision: rendered images are noise-free, so KLT tracking errors are nearly deterministic and repeat
from frame to frame when the view barely changes (hover, slow rotation, slow straight flight); the
filter treats every frame's 1 px measurement as independent and becomes overconfident, mostly in
roll/pitch (accel-bias/tilt), which then lets position drift until a large correction. Fewer frames
= less correlated error per update (c2), and faster rotation renews the view (b). This matches §58
(NEES ≲ 3 at 15 Hz) and day 4 (all frames worse). **Open issue, not fixed** (no noise inflation, no
rate change adopted): candidates are simulated image noise/blur (the stage-5 degradation module), a
correlated-measurement model, or keyframe-style frame selection when parallax is low. The in-flight
failure in slow rotation is a hardware-relevant hazard to test on the real D455 (whose images are
noisy, which may decorrelate the errors).
