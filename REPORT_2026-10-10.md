## Step 1 — ground-truth-free consistency: NIS and innovation whiteness
Scratch OpenVINS build (env-gated prints; pinned fork unchanged): per feature update χ²/dof (MSCKF and
SLAM, before gating) and SLAM features' normalized residuals r/√S_ii. Whiteness = lag-1 Pearson
correlation of a feature's normalized residual across its consecutive updates (pooled over features).
Sim: the 9 sensor flights below, replayed at 15 Hz (system: every other frame) and 5 Hz. EuRoC: all 11
sequences at 20 / 10 / 5 Hz with OpenVINS's EuRoC config + our tracking settings.

| data | rate | runs | lag-1 corr (median [range], mean of u, v) | SLAM NIS/dof median | MSCKF NIS/dof median |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EuRoC | 20 Hz | 9 | **0.76 [0.65–0.88]** | 0.30 | 0.18 |
| EuRoC | 10 Hz | 10 | 0.77 [0.60–0.88] | 0.35 | 0.27 |
| EuRoC | 5 Hz | 9 | 0.65 [0.60–0.84] | 0.45 | 0.35 |
| sim | 15 Hz | 9 | **0.69 [0.65–0.82]** | 0.07 | 0.02 |
| sim | 5 Hz | 9 | 0.58 [0.56–0.66] | 0.07 | 0.04 |

(Runs with < 1000 residual pairs excluded: EuRoC MH_01 20 Hz diverged, MH_04 failed at all rates — counted.)
**The rule's verdict: same pattern → real.** Innovations are strongly correlated in time on real images
too; dropping to 5 Hz reduces it only a little in both. The sim differs in magnitude, not in kind: its
per-frame residuals are ~4x smaller relative to the assumed 1 px (NIS/dof 0.07 vs 0.30), so its
filter is more confident and more exposed to the correlation. Outliers dominate the means in both
(SLAM NIS/dof means 0.6–100).

## Step 2 — mechanism in sim: KLT error against ground truth
For each cam0 track, the 3D point is the first observation's pixel ray cast from the GT camera pose into
the scene geometry; later true pixels are its projections with the GT poses. 9 flights × 2 rates,
textured office and plain office; pooled medians over flights:

| rate | surface | tracks | median \|e\| (px) after 1 / 3 / 5 / 10 / 20 frames | lag-1 corr of per-frame increment (u, v) | lag-1 corr of error (u, v) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 Hz | textured | 44177 | 0.19 / 0.30 / 0.38 / 0.53 / 0.71 | 0.32, 0.07 | 0.99, 0.99 |
| 15 Hz | plain | 2522 | 0.69 / 1.18 / 1.54 / 2.62 / 3.32 | 0.96, 0.44 | 0.99, 0.99 |
| 15 Hz | dim | 5195 | 0.21 / 0.34 / 0.44 / 0.60 / 0.76 | 0.35, −0.01 | 0.98, 0.96 |
| 15 Hz | floor | 11153 | 0.18 / 0.31 / 0.42 / 0.63 / 0.93 | 0.21, 0.10 | 0.99, 0.99 |
| 5 Hz | textured | 33803 | 0.27 / 0.44 / 0.56 / 0.66 / 0.79 | 0.42, 0.24 | 0.93, 0.92 |
| 5 Hz | plain | 1210 | 0.63 / 1.37 / 2.17 / 2.65 / 2.20 | 0.70, 0.60 | 0.98, 0.98 |
| 5 Hz | dim | 4129 | 0.29 / 0.49 / 0.62 / 0.71 / 0.75 | 0.13, 0.05 | 0.88, 0.81 |
| 5 Hz | floor | 9127 | 0.28 / 0.56 / 0.79 / 0.91 / 1.00 | 0.13, 0.20 | 0.87, 0.83 |

KLT error accumulates like a random walk along a track (each frame adds ~0.1–0.2 px, partly correlated
with the previous increment), so a feature's measurement error is nearly the same from one frame to the
next. Over ~1 s of track (15 frames at 15 Hz vs 5 at 5 Hz) the error is about the same (textured ~0.6 px),
so the higher rate supplies 3x more updates carrying the same error — the filter counts them as
independent. On plain texture the drift is 3–4x faster and the increments themselves are correlated
(0.96). Dimming barely matters. Step 1 shows real images carry the same correlation structure; the
sim mainly makes the per-frame error smaller relative to the assumed 1 px.

## Step 3 — decisive drift test: does covariance growth track drift when the filter is consistent?
10 sensor flights (no depth recorded; office and plain office; frontier and pose_cov λ = 0 mixed;
current executor). 9 usable: **d7_sens_tex_fr_2 failed** (climb timeout at OpenVINS z 0.46 m, landed;
cause in "What needs you"). All 9: armed on the first command, no VIO-gate openings, no contacts,
disarmed by the executor at touchdown (5 by PX4 first, 4 by the OpenVINS backup). Each replayed at 15
and 5 Hz (deterministic serial runner), then the day-6 step-4 analysis on the replay estimates.

| | 15 Hz (system) | 5 Hz |
|---|---|---|
| roll/pitch NEES, median over flights [range] | 5.3 [3.2–10.5] | **3.2 [1.9–4.3]** (2 DoF) |
| orientation NEES | 3.9 [2.7–5.7] | 1.8 [1.5–2.6] |
| windows (20 s, ≥ 1 m) | 133 | 114 |
| GT position drift per metre, median | 3.4 mm/m | 3.3 mm/m |
| **Spearman(σ_pos growth, position drift)** | 0.04 [−0.12, 0.24] | **0.03 [−0.11, 0.19]** |
| **Spearman(σ_yaw growth, yaw drift)** | 0.20 [−0.05, 0.41] | **0.47 [0.23, 0.60]** |

At 5 Hz the filter is close to consistent by NEES, yet its position covariance growth is unrelated to
the realized position drift, and yaw stays below the ≥ 0.5 bar (CI touches it). Lowering the rate
improves calibration of the *level* (NEES) but not the *ranking* across paths. With step 1 showing the
same correlated innovations on real data, this is not a sim fidelity problem that a rate workaround
would fix.
